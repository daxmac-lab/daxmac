Developer Implementation Review · Area 05

Chat vs. Voice Testing

Voice AI Platform

The question

What changes in a simulation when the same assistant is tested through chat versus voice, and what does each mode actually validate?

What we investigated

We compared how the same assistant and scenario are exercised through chat and voice simulation modes, focusing on the implementation surfaces each mode actually tests.

What we found

Chat and voice simulations exercise overlapping conversational behavior, but they do not validate the same implementation surface.

Chat tests the conversation without audio. Voice additionally exercises speech recognition, voice output, pronunciation, pacing, and turn-taking.

A successful chat simulation therefore does not establish that the configured voice implementation works correctly.

Why it matters

A developer can see a successful chat result and conclude that the assistant is ready for voice interaction without having tested the audio layer that the voice experience depends on.

A voice simulation provides additional evidence, but still does not establish behavior across every real caller or audio environment.

Evidence

A controlled comparison of the same assistant and scenario across chat and voice showed that the two modes exercise different parts of the implementation.

Result

The chat result validates the conversation without audio. The voice result adds evidence about the speech and turn-taking layers that chat does not exercise.

Implementation implication

Developers need to distinguish what a chat result validates from what requires voice testing, and what a voice simulation still leaves to real-world validation.

What this demonstrates

DaxMac investigates what each testing mode actually validates, including the implementation surfaces that a successful result does and does not cover.
