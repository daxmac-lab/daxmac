Monitoring & Observability Developer Onboarding Audit

Live audit. Company identity withheld for privacy.

Finding: A Check can make monitoring coverage feel more complete than what the platform actually takes responsibility for.

Finding

The onboarding presents Checks as automated monitoring that executes a configured workflow, retries failures, stores the result, and alerts when the final attempt fails.

It also describes Checks as experiencing the application in a way comparable to real users.

The experience therefore makes the operational side of monitoring feel largely handled by the platform: the Check runs, evaluates the configured flow, records the result, and reports failures.

The important boundary is that the developer still determines what the Check actually verifies.

Developer Assumption

A developer can reasonably come away believing:

"Once I put an important application flow into a Check, the platform is continuously validating that part of my application."

The deeper assumption is that the platform has taken responsibility for monitoring the reliability of that application behavior, rather than primarily executing the monitoring logic the developer has defined.

Where the Boundary Appears

The platform absorbs the operational work of running the Check.

The developer remains responsible for defining the workflow, assertions, authentication behavior, data interactions, and other test logic that determine what the Check actually covers.

This creates an important distinction:

Check execution ≠ complete monitoring coverage.

A Check can be running continuously while still representing only the portion of production behavior that the developer has chosen to encode.

Production Trigger

The application changes, or a production failure occurs outside what the configured Check actually exercises.

The Check can continue executing successfully because the behavior it was designed to verify still passes, while an important production behavior remains outside its coverage.

Trust Consequence

A consistently passing Check can create confidence that an important production flow is protected when significant failure conditions may still sit outside its configured coverage.

The risk is therefore not that the Check fails to run.

The risk is that successful monitoring execution can be interpreted as broader application protection than the configured Check actually provides.

Possible Interventions

Make the boundary between Check execution and monitoring coverage explicit.

Clarify that the reliability of the monitoring result depends on how completely the developer models the production behavior being monitored.

Show examples of what should be included in a production-critical Check, including relevant assertions and failure conditions.

Explain the difference between a Check passing and the broader application behavior being healthy.

Scope

This finding does not claim that the monitoring platform promises complete application coverage.

The finding is narrower: the onboarding experience can cause developers to associate an active, consistently passing Check with broader application validation than the configured monitoring logic necessarily represents.

Trust Risk Medium · Priority High

The consequence is primarily a monitoring-confidence risk rather than direct data loss or service failure. However, the assumption can matter in production when teams rely on passing Checks as evidence that an important application flow remains healthy.
