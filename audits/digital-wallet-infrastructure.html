Finding: A notification abstraction can make customer delivery appear more reliable than it is.

Finding

The onboarding presents notifications as a way to reach customers at the right moment directly through Apple Wallet and Google Wallet.

It explains how to send notifications, including links, dynamic pass data, membership expiry, and account activity.

The API also returns a notification status of "delivered" with the message "Delivered succesfully" after sending.

The experience therefore makes the notification operation feel closely connected to the customer-facing delivery of the message.

The important boundary is that successful submission through the platform does not determine whether or when the underlying wallet platform makes that notification visible to the customer.

Developer Assumption

A developer can reasonably come away believing:

"If the notification was successfully sent, I can rely on it as the customer communication channel."

The deeper assumption is that the platform has absorbed responsibility for getting the customer-facing message to the passholder, rather than primarily handling the mechanics of submitting the notification to the underlying wallet platforms.

Where the Boundary Appears

The platform absorbs the mechanics of sending the notification to Apple Wallet and Google Wallet.

The final user-visible experience remains dependent on platform-specific behavior, including notification timing, frequency limits, permissions, and wallet state.

Apple may suppress notifications sent within very short intervals and can rate-limit notifications.

Google does not display notifications in real time and typically takes between 10 seconds and 2 minutes. Google also limits push notifications to 3 per day per pass; exceeding that limit means the pass can still be updated without producing a lock-screen push.

This creates an important distinction:

Successful notification submission ≠ guaranteed customer-visible delivery.

Production Trigger

A developer uses a notification for a time-sensitive customer update and treats a successful notification response as confirmation that the customer has received the communication.

The notification can be successfully submitted while the underlying wallet platform delays, suppresses, limits, or otherwise prevents the message from appearing to the customer as expected.

Trust Consequence

A successful notification operation can create confidence that an important customer communication has reached the user when the final user-visible outcome remains dependent on Apple or Google Wallet behavior.

The risk is therefore not that the notification API fails to submit the request.

The risk is that successful submission can be interpreted as broader delivery reliability than the underlying wallet platforms actually provide.

Possible Interventions

Make successful submission distinct from customer-visible delivery.

Clearly establish the platform-specific conditions that determine whether and when a notification reaches the user.

Highlight timing, frequency limits, permissions, and other wallet-level constraints that can affect visibility.

Clarify that time-sensitive communication should not automatically treat a successful notification response as confirmation of immediate customer visibility.

DaxMac Finding

The notification abstraction can make the platform's delivery responsibility appear broader than what the underlying wallet platforms actually guarantee.

Scope

This finding does not claim that the platform guarantees that every notification will be displayed immediately to a customer.

The finding is narrower: the onboarding experience can cause developers to associate successful notification submission with reliable customer-facing delivery, even though the final visible outcome remains dependent on the underlying wallet platforms.

Trust Risk Medium · Priority High

The consequence is primarily a communication-reliability risk. It becomes more significant for time-sensitive customer communication, where a successful API operation can create confidence that the customer was reached while final user-visible delivery remains outside complete control.
