The Problem

Your product experience teaches developers how to implement it.

Developers don't only follow instructions. They build a mental model of what the product handles, what they need to configure, what happens automatically, and what responsibility remains theirs.

01

What they see

The documentation, product presentation, examples, defaults, and implementation flow they encounter.

02

What they assume

The responsibilities and behaviors they reasonably believe the product handles for them.

03

What they implement

The implementation decisions they make based on that mental model.

04

What actually happens

The responsibilities, conditions, or behaviors that remain once the implementation meets the real product.

When those don't line up, the resulting problem can surface as support questions, engineering work, failed implementation, or production surprises.

How It Works

We investigate where implementation assumptions form.

DaxMac follows the implementation experience from the developer's perspective, identifies the assumptions it can naturally create, and evaluates where those assumptions can create downstream risk.

01

02

Observed Behavior

Some assumptions need to be tested against the product.

Some implementation risks can be identified from the developer experience alone. Others depend on how the product actually behaves when the relevant implementation path is exercised.

01

Developer experience

What the developer is shown throughout the implementation journey.

02

Developer expectation

What the experience can reasonably lead the developer to believe.

03

Implementation attempt

The relevant implementation path is exercised against the actual product where necessary.

04

Observed behavior

What the product actually does under the tested conditions.

05

Validated finding

A documented gap between the reasonable expectation and observed behavior.

Live testing is included only where observed product behavior is necessary to establish the finding. It is not general QA.

What We Look For

Not a documentation review.

DaxMac isn't primarily asking whether your documentation is clear. The question is what a developer can reasonably believe after moving through the implementation experience.

A product experience can be technically accurate and still create an incorrect implementation model. DaxMac looks for the gaps between what the experience appears to promise, what the developer is likely to assume, and what implementation actually leaves to the customer.
