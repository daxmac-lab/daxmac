About DaxMac

We examine how customers actually implement developer-facing products.

DaxMac helps developer-facing companies identify implementation gaps and potential risks by independently examining how customers work through their products.

A developer builds an understanding of a product from the APIs, SDKs, examples, configuration, interfaces, and behavior they encounter. That understanding can be reasonable but incomplete or incorrect.

When an implementation assumption doesn't match what the product actually requires or does, the consequences can extend beyond the developer. They may appear as failed integrations, additional support requests, engineering escalations, implementation delays, or production issues.

DaxMac examines these implementation paths from the customer's perspective, providing evidence-backed findings that help teams understand where implementation can break down and prioritize concerns that warrant attention.

What DaxMac Does

DaxMac examines how a developer would implement a developer-facing product to achieve a specific result.

We take a defined implementation path—such as integrating an API, configuring an SDK, setting up a deployment, connecting a tool, or testing a workflow—and work through it from the customer's side.

We examine what the product leads the developer to believe, what the implementation actually requires, and where those two can diverge.

Where necessary, we validate behavior directly through the API, SDK, product, or deployment workflow rather than relying only on what the product says should happen.

The objective is to identify the assumptions, gaps, and potential risks that may otherwise be difficult for internal teams to recognize, then explain why the findings matter and what the team could consider addressing.

What DaxMac Helps Companies Address

Implementation difficulties don't always indicate that a product is broken. A product can work as designed while customers struggle to understand how to implement it correctly.

For example, a developer may misunderstand a configuration requirement, overlook a resource dependency, misinterpret authentication behavior, or miss a step needed to move an integration into production.

Depending on the product and implementation path, these gaps can contribute to:

  • Failed or incomplete integrations
  • Repeated implementation questions and additional support requests
  • Engineering escalations
  • Delayed implementations and customer onboarding
  • Broken customer workflows
  • Production-readiness issues
  • Difficulty adopting or using the product as intended

DaxMac helps companies investigate these implementation concerns, understand their potential consequences, and prioritize which findings deserve attention.

These are potential consequences to investigate, not outcomes that every review will necessarily uncover.

How We Investigate

We don't start by asking whether a product's documentation is well written. We start with the implementation itself.

A Developer Implementation Review can involve:

  • Following the customer path. We work through the steps a developer would take to implement the product and achieve the intended result.
  • Examining the relevant product surfaces. We investigate the APIs, SDKs, configuration, examples, workflows, product behavior, and other elements the developer relies on.
  • Validating behavior. Where the implementation depends on actual system behavior, we can test it directly through the product, API, SDK, or deployment workflow.
  • Tracing implementation responsibilities. We identify what the product handles, what the customer must configure or manage, and where an incorrect assumption can enter the implementation.
  • Documenting the evidence. We connect findings to the relevant implementation steps and the evidence used to establish them.

The scope depends on the implementation path and the questions the review needs to answer. Live testing and other validation activities are included when appropriate to the agreed scope.

What a Developer Implementation Review Produces

The client receives a documented assessment of the implementation path examined and the concerns identified within it.

Depending on the agreed scope, deliverables can include:

  • A mapped developer implementation path
  • Relevant product, API, and SDK sources
  • Findings supported by implementation evidence
  • Controlled validation and live API or SDK testing
  • Screenshots and relevant requests and responses
  • JSON, code, and test configuration where applicable
  • An explanation of implementation assumptions and potential consequences
  • Prioritized findings and directions for addressing identified gaps

Each finding connects the implementation step, the assumption a developer could reasonably make, the available evidence, and the potential consequences.

The result is more than a list of observations. It gives product and engineering teams an independent assessment they can use to evaluate implementation concerns and decide which ones warrant further investigation or remediation.

The purpose isn't to produce another general review of the product. It's to establish where the customer implementation can break down, why, and what that could mean for the company.

Why I Built DaxMac

Developer-facing products are built by people who understand how they work. Their customers approach them from the outside.

Customers make implementation decisions based on the interfaces, behavior, examples, configuration, and information available to them. They don't automatically share the assumptions or internal knowledge of the people who built the product.

That difference can create implementation gaps even when the product works as intended.

I built DaxMac to examine that gap independently, from the customer's side. The goal is to understand how developers interpret a product, what they need to do to implement it successfully, and where the available evidence reveals concerns worth addressing.

The aim is to help companies identify and evaluate implementation risks before those risks create further customer or engineering problems.

Find the implementation problems before customers have to find them for you.
